Welcome & Your Starting Point

The most useful lesson in a small starting account is not that $500 magically becomes a fortune. It is that a small amount can become the beginning of a repeatable system.

The Habit Principle

A contribution you can repeat is more useful than a contribution that looks impressive once. Your first goal is to create sustainable investing behavior.

Section 5

How to Read a Chart

A price chart is a record of the tug-of-war between buyers and sellers. Once you know the basic vocabulary, most charts start to tell a simple story.

Anatomy of a Candlestick

Each candle shows four prices for one time period: open, high, low, and close. The thin wicks mark the high and low; the thick body marks the range between open and close.

Support & Resistance

Resistance is a price level a stock struggles to break above; support is a level it tends to bounce off of. Watch what happens when price revisits these levels - a clean break can signal the next move.

Don't Trade the Chart Alone

Chart patterns describe what has already happened - they don't guarantee what happens next. Use them alongside the research process from Section 3, not as a replacement for it.

Section 6

Options 101

An option is a contract, not a stock. It gives you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell 100 shares of a company at an agreed price, by an agreed date.

Call Option

Gives you the right to buy 100 shares at the strike price. Buyers typically want the stock to go up.

Put Option

Gives you the right to sell 100 shares at the strike price. Buyers typically want the stock to go down.

Strike Price - the fixed price at which the contract lets you buy (call) or sell (put) the stock.
Premium - the price you pay to buy the contract. This is the most a buyer can lose.
Expiration Date - the last date the contract is valid. After this, it is either exercised or expires worthless.
In / At / Out of the Money - whether the strike price is currently favorable (ITM), right at the stock price (ATM), or unfavorable (OTM) if exercised today.
Intrinsic Value - the real, built-in profit an option already has if exercised right now. Never below zero.
Time (Extrinsic) Value - the rest of the premium, reflecting the chance the option becomes more valuable before it expires. Shrinks to zero by expiration.

Why This Matters for a $500 Account

Options add leverage - bigger potential gains, but the whole premium can be lost if the stock doesn't move your way in time. Most beginners are better off building the share-by-share habit from Sections 1-3 first, and treating this section as background knowledge for later, not a starting strategy.
